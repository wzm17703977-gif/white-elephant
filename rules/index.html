White Elephant Rules

Everything you need to run a white elephant gift exchange: the simple classic rules, the dice version, tips for big groups and a few twists. Print it and tape it to the wall.

The simple rules (classic version)

  1. Set a budget. Everyone brings one wrapped gift worth about the same amount (for example $25). No names on the gifts.
  2. Make a pile. Put all the gifts in the middle.
  3. Draw numbers to decide the order. Use paper slips, printable number cards or the number generator.
  4. Player 1 opens a gift from the pile and shows it to everyone.
  5. Every next player chooses: open a new gift from the pile or steal a gift someone has already opened.
  6. If your gift is stolen, you choose again: open a new gift or steal a different opened gift.
  7. The game ends when the last wrapped gift is opened.

The three house rules most groups add

  • Steal limit: a gift can be stolen at most three times. After the third steal it is “frozen” and stays put.
  • No instant steal-back: you can’t immediately take back the gift that was just stolen from you.
  • Final swap for player 1: since player 1 never got to choose, they may take one last turn at the end and swap with anyone.

White elephant rules with dice

Everyone starts holding a wrapped gift. Take turns rolling one die and do what the number says. Start a timer (10 minutes is plenty for 15 people); when it ends, everyone unwraps what they’re holding.

1Open your gift (or take one from the pile)
2Swap gifts with the person on your left
3Swap gifts with the person on your right
4Steal any gift you like
5Swap with someone across the room
6Pass — keep what you have

Roll on your phone with the online white elephant dice game — it has a timer and lets you edit every move.

Rules for large groups (20+ people)

  • Lower the steal limit to two so the game doesn’t drag.
  • Add a time limit per turn — 30 seconds to decide keeps things moving.
  • Split into tables of 10–15 people, each with its own pile, if you have more than 40.
  • Show the order on a screen so nobody loses track of whose turn it is.

Twists to keep it fun

  • Theme night: everything must be “something red”, “a book” or “made in your state”.
  • Re-gift round: only re-gifted items allowed — the weirder the better.
  • Mystery steal: stolen gifts must stay wrapped until the end (works best with the dice version).
  • Left-right story: read a short story out loud; every time you hear “left” or “right”, pass your gift that way.

White elephant vs Yankee swap vs Dirty Santa

Same game, different names. “White elephant” is the most common name in the US, “Yankee swap” is used in New England and “Dirty Santa” in the South. Some groups use “Dirty Santa” only for the version where stealing is encouraged.

FAQ

How much should a white elephant gift cost?

Whatever your group agrees on. Offices often pick $20–$30; friends and families $10–$25. The important part is that everyone uses the same budget.

Can you steal a gift that was just stolen?

Yes — anyone except the person it was just taken from. Most groups only block the immediate steal-back.

What if someone doesn’t bring a gift?

Let them play but go last, or have the host keep one or two spare gifts in the pile.

Is white elephant the same as Secret Santa?

No. In Secret Santa each person is secretly assigned someone to buy for. Running one? Use the Secret Santa generator (no email needed).
